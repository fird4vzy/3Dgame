# 22 — Main menu, transitions, gamepad menus, ambience

The game had no front door and no memory. Both fixed.

## The bug that mattered most: the game never loaded its save

`QuestSystem.restore()`, `IlluminationSystem.restore()` and
`ShardSystem.restore()` all existed, were unit-tested, and **nothing called
them**. Progress was written to `localStorage` on every delivery and silently
discarded on the next visit. A returning player found a save file full of
progress and a world that had forgotten all of it.

That is the failure mode good unit tests do not catch: every piece worked in
isolation, nothing connected them.

`PlanetScene.restoreProgress()` now runs at boot and restores **silently** —
districts snap to lit with no ignition animation and no `delivery:completed`
events replay. A returning player should find the world as they left it, not sit
through five cutscenes.

A second bug lived in the same path: `SoundBoard.startMusic()` passed **district
ids** to a music director that keys on **stem names**. `landing` is not `bass`,
so restoring a save produced no music at all. The mapping lives in the district
data and now gets looked up.

## Main menu

Rendered over the live planet in a slow high orbit. The world is already loaded,
so it previews the reward state for the cost of one camera path — and with a
save present you can see your own lit districts turning behind the menu.

**Continue is primary whenever a save exists.** After launch, returning players
outnumber first runs, and making them hunt past "New run" every time is the
small rudeness menus are usually guilty of.

Starting a fresh run resets in place (`QuestSystem.reset()`,
`IlluminationSystem.reset()`, `ShardSystem.reset()`, each symmetrical with its
`restore()`), rather than reloading the page — a reload would drop the player
back on the menu instead of into the run they just asked for.

## Iris transitions

A DOM overlay, not a shader pass: costs nothing on the GPU, covers HUD and menus
too, and keeps working if the render loop stalls mid-load — which is exactly
when a transition is on screen. The iris closes over the menu, the world changes
behind it, and it opens on the surface, so the cut from orbit to ground is never
seen. `prefers-reduced-motion` collapses it to a cross-fade.

## Gamepad menus

Spatial focus movement, not tab order: distance along the axis is the cost,
drift across it penalised 2.5×, so "down" lands on what is visually below.
Sliders adjust on left/right instead of moving focus. The scoring is a pure
function with its own tests — the same discipline that finally pinned the A/D
handedness bug.

## Ambience

Five district beds, generated by the same tool as the music. `AmbienceDirector`
is the music director's shape again: every bed starts at once and crossfades,
**never starts on entry** — otherwise every boundary crossing fires an audible
attack, and on a planet you can circle in ninety seconds that is constant.

`seamless()` cross-fades each bed's tail over its head with an equal-power
curve. Noise beds are the one place a loop discontinuity is unmissable, and a
linear cross-fade dips in the middle and reads as a dropout.

## Two visual fixes

- The menu panel was centred because `justify-items` does not move a single grid
  child; `justify-content` does.
- A hard **rectangular seam** crossed the planet in the menu. The shadow frustum
  is sized tightly around the player for gameplay, and from an orbital camera
  its boundary is visible. Shadows now switch off in menu mode.

## Test note

The smoke suite's jump assertion had been measuring terrain luck. It pressed
Space while still sprinting, and running over rough ground leaves the character
airborne often enough that the press lands outside coyote time and is swallowed
— correct behaviour, wrong test. It now stops and waits to be grounded before
jumping, which is what "test the jump" means.

## Verification

164 unit tests, both browser suites green. 2.43 MB gzipped against 6 MB, of
which 1.99 MB is audio that loads after boot.
